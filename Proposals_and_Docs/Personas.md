# **Personas \- Flexibly Adaptive Translation**

## **1\. Objective and design principles**

seven content attributes that can be controlled independently:

1. Profanity  
2. Verbal aggression  
3. Sexual explicitness  
4. Violence and graphicness  
5. Substance-use depiction  
6. Horror or disturbing imagery  
7. Discriminatory or identity-directed language

0–1 scale for each attribute:

| Score | Interpretation |
| ----- | ----- |
| 0.0 | No or minimal presence of the attribute |
| 0.2 | Very mild |
| 0.4 | Mild to moderate |
| 0.6 | Moderate to strong |
| 0.8 | Strong |
| 1.0 | Very strong; close to the maximum intensity the content can reasonably express |

The profiles distinguish three viewing contexts:

* **Family:** watching with family or relatives, potentially including different generations.  
* **Friends:** watching with peers or a chosen social group.  
* **Individual:** watching alone, without the need to accommodate a shared audience.


### **Persona 1: Aditi Sharma \- The Multigenerational Family Viewer**

**Background**

* **Age:** 21  
* **Location:** Jaipur, Rajasthan  
* **Occupation:** Undergraduate student  
* **Family:** Lives with parents and grandparents in a multigenerational household.  
* **Media habits:** Enjoys international films, Hindi cinema, romantic comedies and thrillers. Often watches movies with her family on weekends.  
* **Personal outlook:** Comfortable with mature themes and realistic storytelling, but prefers less explicit dialogue when watching with older relatives.

**Preferences**

Aditi does not necessarily dislike profanity or sexual references. Her preference changes because she wants everyone in the room to enjoy the movie without awkwardness. When alone, she would rather preserve the director's original dialogue than have it sanitized.

| Attribute | Family | Friends | Individual |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Profanity | 0.2 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Verbal aggression | 0.4 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Sexual explicitness | 0.1 | 0.6 | 0.8 |
| Violence and graphicness | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Substance-use depiction | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Horror or disturbing imagery | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.9 |
| Discriminatory language | 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.4 |

**Cultural rendering preference**

* **Family:** Explain unfamiliar references and localize idioms when doing so makes dialogue easier for everyone to follow.  
* **Friends:** Preserve cultural references, including unfamiliar ones.  
* **Individual:** Preserve the original cultural context and wordplay as much as possible.

**Research hypothesis:** Aditi demonstrates *context-dependent adaptation*. The same user has substantially different target settings depending on the audience, even though her personal identity remains unchanged.

### **Persona 2: Arjun Mehta \- The Unfiltered Cinema Enthusiast**

**Background**

* **Age:** 25  
* **Location:** Mumbai, Maharashtra  
* **Occupation:** Film editor  
* **Family:** Lives independently; visits his family occasionally.  
* **Media habits:** Watches international crime dramas, independent cinema, psychological thrillers and dark comedies.  
* **Personal outlook:** Values artistic intent, realistic dialogue and character authenticity. Dislikes translations that make morally complex characters sound artificially polite.

**Preferences**

Arjun considers profanity, aggression and disturbing imagery legitimate storytelling devices. He wants the translated dialogue to retain a character's personality, even when that personality is unpleasant.

However, he distinguishes between depicting discriminatory language and endorsing it. He does not want hateful dialogue unnecessarily amplified or casually inserted where it did not exist.

**Cultural rendering preference**

* **Family:** Explain references that would otherwise make the narrative difficult to follow, without unnecessarily simplifying the story.  
* **Friends:** Preserve cultural references and stylistic nuances.  
* **Individual:** Prefer preservation of the original cultural context, including region-specific humour and references.

**Research hypothesis:** Arjun tests the upper end of the attribute controls. His profile can reveal whether the system can preserve high-intensity content without accidentally increasing unrelated attributes or changing narrative meaning.

### **Persona 3: Fatima Khan \- The Parent Watching With Young Children**

**Background**

* **Age:** 34  
* **Location:** Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh  
* **Occupation:** Schoolteacher  
* **Family:** Married, with children aged 6 and 9\.  
* **Media habits:** Enjoys animated films, adventure movies, mysteries and selected mainstream dramas.  
* **Personal outlook:** Wants entertaining content that children can watch comfortably, but does not want every difficult subject removed from a film.

**What influences her preferences?**

Fatima distinguishes between her personal preferences and her children's viewing needs. She is comfortable discussing serious subjects with adults but wants to avoid highly explicit sexual content, graphic violence and intense profanity when watching with her children.

She may accept a film containing danger or conflict if the presentation is not excessively graphic.

| Attribute | Family with children | Friends | Individual |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Profanity | 0.0 | 0.6 | 0.7 |
| Verbal aggression | 0.2 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Sexual explicitness | 0.0 | 0.5 | 0.7 |
| Violence and graphicness | 0.1 | 0.6 | 0.8 |
| Substance-use depiction | 0.1 | 0.6 | 0.8 |
| Horror or disturbing imagery | 0.1 | 0.6 | 0.8 |
| Discriminatory language | 0.0 | 0.3 | 0.4 |

**Cultural rendering preference**

* **Family with children:** Explain unfamiliar cultural concepts using accessible language while preserving the story's essential meaning.  
* **Friends:** Preserve references or explain them where necessary.  
* **Individual:** Prefer cultural preservation and natural, contextually accurate dialogue.

**Research hypothesis:** Fatima tests whether the system can generate a child-oriented rendering without treating all mature themes as interchangeable. For example, a scene could retain moderate peril while reducing graphic violence and profanity independently.

**Important distinction:** This profile represents a parent's chosen settings for a particular viewing context, not a universal definition of child suitability. Actual children's needs also vary by age, maturity and individual sensitivities.

### **Persona 8: Tashi Lepcha \- The Viewer Who Values Regional and Cultural Specificity**

**Background**

* **Age:** 24  
* **Location:** Gangtok, Sikkim  
* **Occupation:** Graduate student  
* **Family:** Maintains close connections with family and friends from different linguistic backgrounds.  
* **Media habits:** Enjoys regional Indian cinema, international films, folklore-based stories and historical dramas.  
* **Personal outlook:** Comfortable with a wide range of content intensities. Particularly dislikes translations that replace culturally specific references with generic or stereotypically Indian equivalents.

**What influences his preferences?**

Tashi's defining preference is cultural rather than necessarily content-related.

He wants dialogue to retain the identities, social relationships and cultural context of the characters. If a character refers to a local custom, regional expression or culturally specific practice, he generally prefers that reference to survive translation.

He does not necessarily want every foreign reference explained or converted into something familiar to the target audience.

| Horror or disturbing imagery | 0.4 | 0.8 | 0.9 |
| Discriminatory language | 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.4 |

**Cultural rendering preference**

* **Family:** Preserve cultural references; explain them when the audience might otherwise miss their significance.

### **Persona 9: Priya \- The Viewer Who Prefers Contextual Handling of Discriminatory Language**

**Background**

* **Age:** 29  
* **Location:** Kochi, Kerala  
* **Occupation:** Social researcher  
* **Family:** Lives independently and regularly watches films with a close group of friends.  
* **Media habits:** Enjoys political dramas, historical films, documentaries, satire and character-driven cinema.  
* **Personal outlook:** Comfortable with profanity, strong arguments and mature themes. Particularly attentive to how caste, gender, ethnicity, religion and other identities are represented in media.

**What influences her preferences?**

Priya's preference is not simply to eliminate all identity-related language.

She distinguishes between:

* A character directing a slur at someone.  
* A historical film depicting discriminatory attitudes.  
* A satirical scene criticizing prejudice.  
* A character discussing discrimination in a serious or educational context.

The same expression can perform very different narrative functions in these situations.

She wants the translation to preserve those distinctions while avoiding gratuitous amplification of harmful language.

| Attribute | Family | Friends | Individual |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Profanity | 0.3 | 0.8 | 0.9 |
| Verbal aggression | 0.4 | 0.8 | 0.9 |
| Sexual explicitness | 0.2 | 0.6 | 0.7 |
| Violence and graphicness | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Substance-use depiction | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Horror or disturbing imagery | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Discriminatory language | 0.0 | 0.1 | 0.2 |

**Cultural rendering preference**

* **Family:** Explain culturally specific references when necessary, especially when their significance might otherwise be lost.  
* **Friends:** Preserve references and their narrative function.  
* **Individual:** Prefer preservation of cultural and historical context, with careful handling of identity-related language.

**Research hypothesis:** Priya tests whether the system can distinguish the intensity of discriminatory language from its communicative function.

A successful transformation should not accidentally make an anti-discrimination scene appear to endorse the prejudice it depicts, nor erase the historical significance of a discriminatory expression.

He prefers the translation to preserve the comedic intent without necessarily preserving every literal expression. He also wants jokes targeting identity groups to retain their actual narrative function rather than being indiscriminately intensified or sanitized.

## **3\. Comparative overview of the personas**

The ten profiles cover different reasons why an audience might request a particular rendering.

| Persona | Primary preference driver | Most informative attribute controls | Most informative cultural control |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 1\. Aditi | Multigenerational viewing | Profanity, sexual explicitness | Explain/localize |
| 2\. Arjun | Artistic authenticity | Profanity, aggression, violence | Preserve |
| 3\. Fatima | Viewing with young children | All attributes, especially sexual explicitness and graphicness | Explain |
| 4\. Rohan | Cross-cultural familiarity | Broad tolerance for mature content; careful discrimination handling | Preserve |
| 5\. Meera | Personal preference for low explicitness | Profanity, sexual explicitness, graphicness | Explain/preserve |
| 6\. Kabir | Low tolerance for graphic violence | Violence and horror, independently of profanity | Preserve |
| 7\. Zoya | Selective content preferences | Sexual explicitness and substance use | Explain/preserve |
| 8\. Tashi | Cultural identity and specificity | Content controls independent of cultural rendering | Preserve |
| 9\. Priya | Contextual handling of discrimination | Discriminatory language and aggression | Preserve/explain |
| 10\. Dev | Social context and humour | Profanity, aggression, sexual explicitness, discriminatory language | Localize/preserve |

Examples:

* Watching alone.  
* Watching with close friends.  
* Watching with parents and grandparents.  
* Watching with children aged 6–9.
